# Make the organization, not the campaign, compound

The best growth system for Lynx is a **hybrid product-led motion built around durable organizations rather than individual campaigns**. Concretely, that means four things. First, self-serve org creation for small races and groups, with human sales triggered by product usage for parties, PACs and unions. Second, one inherent viral loop: an admin can't canvass without inviting volunteers, and those volunteers carry Lynx to their next campaign. Third, a "between cycles" archive tier that keeps an org's data alive, so that churn after election day turns into reactivation in the next cycle. Fourth, hybrid pricing (an org platform fee, per-project add-ons, and metered AI and messaging) so revenue rises with campaign intensity. The evidence says churn in this market is structural, not a product failure. More than half of political-tech vendors themselves turned over between 2022 and 2024, and no vendor publishes customer churn, so cycle-end loss has to be designed for rather than "fixed". Realistic targets for a product priced under $12k a year are about 90% gross revenue retention (GRR) and 100–105% net revenue retention (NRR), measured on a full-cycle basis. Enterprise-style 120% NRR headlines don't apply. Virality should be treated as an amplifier (K roughly 0.2–0.5), not an engine. Federal election law constrains the playbook in one specific way: **any free tier, discount or referral credit offered to campaigns must be offered on identical terms to non-political customers**. Lynx's nonprofit segment is therefore a legal necessity for growth, not only a market. In what follows, sourced facts carry inline citations. Sentences and paragraphs labelled **Inference** are this report's analysis and have not been independently verified.

## Election-cycle churn is structural, and survivors attach to durable assets

Political tech is a hard market to compound in. Swasey's analysis of the Higher Ground Labs landscape counted about 120 companies in 2022 and about 148 in 2024, with roughly **87 entering and 75 exiting, "over half of the set of companies churning in just 2 years"**. In the same piece, Swasey says revenue spikes every four years with smaller spikes every two, and that vendors "can't be 100% sure they'll be paid before clients shut down" ([Swasey](https://cauldronllc.substack.com/p/an-avalanche-of-political-tech)). Hustle cut about **35% of staff after the 2018 midterms**; its CEO said it had been premature to expand without building "beyond politics and nonprofits" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=1768957)). Campaign tech still takes only **1–10% of campaign budgets**, but there has been a **15% rise since 2020 in campaigns buying tech earlier in the cycle**, and down-ballot and first-time candidates "struggle with tool selection and implementation" ([HGL 2024 survey](https://highergroundlabs.com/takeaways-from-our-2024-election-tech-survey/)). No vendor in the research publishes a customer-level post-November churn rate, so the size of the cliff is unknown.

The incumbents that survive do one of five things:

- **Sell to durable organizations.** Solidarity Tech targets labor and grassroots organizers ([Solidarity Tech](https://www.solidarity.tech/pricing)), and Impactive runs a dedicated unions page ([Impactive](https://impactive.io/solutions-for/unions)).
- **Attach to an asset that grows across cycles.** Access to the DNC voter file "got even more valuable over time" ([The Connector](https://theconnector.substack.com/p/living-with-vanxiety-the-present)). About **14.7M ActBlue Express donors** have saved payment information ([ActBlue Express](https://actblue.com/express), search summary).
- **Charge a percentage of money raised.** ActBlue charges 3.95% and WinRed 3.94% ([HuffPost via Yahoo](https://sg.news.yahoo.com/much-touted-trump-era-fundraising-130000421.html)).
- **Get acquired by a durable platform.** ActBlue bought Impactive in September 2025 ([Seedtable](https://seedtable.com/exits/impactive)), and EveryAction bought Mobilize in 2020 ([Mobilize](https://join.mobilize.us/blog/mobilize-joins-everyaction)).
- **Expand internationally.** Qomon raised €5.5M in 2025 to become the "default mobilisation stack for volunteer-powered organisations" across Europe and North America ([Vestbee](https://vestbee.com/insights/articles/qomon-secures-5-5-m)).

Incumbent weakness creates openings. Bonterra (owner of NGP VAN, EveryAction, ActionKit and Mobilize) cut more than 200 staff in September 2023 and labelled ActionKit "maintain" ([The Intercept](https://theintercept.com/2023/10/05/democrats-campaign-tech-layoffs-2024-bonterra-ngp-van-actionkit/)). MoveOn then left ActionKit after 25 years, citing PE consolidation and "repeated company layoffs" ([The Connector](https://theconnector.substack.com/p/news-the-post-24-democratic-tech)). VAN "faced stability concerns requiring outside intervention during peak campaign moments" in 2024 ([C&E](https://campaignsandelections.com/industry-news/report-urges-democrats-to-rethink-tech/)). Trust also depends on partisan alignment. Hustle's owner hosted a $12M Trump fundraiser in 2024, which angered Democratic clients; the company changed CEO and employees bought it in 2026 ([Wikipedia: Hustle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hustle_(company))).

**Inference:** Lynx already bills the org rather than the campaign. Its no-delete invariant and append-only `canvass_visits`, donor links and door-attribute history are exactly the kind of within-org asset that accumulates across cycles. The strategic job is to make that asset visibly valuable on the morning after an election, when the next candidate, ballot measure or issue campaign is deciding which tool to use. Three further implications follow:

- **Reliability during GOTV week is itself a retention event.** VAN's 2024 instability and Bonterra's cuts both drove defections.
- **Ownership and mission are selling points.** Lynx should state its partisan or nonpartisan stance explicitly rather than leave buyers guessing.
- **Don't staff for the peak.** Hustle and WinRed (which lost about $6M off-cycle, per [HuffPost](https://sg.news.yahoo.com/much-touted-trump-era-fundraising-130000421.html)) show that fixed costs sized for a peak become losses in the trough.

## Hybrid product-led sales beats both pure PLG and pure field sales

The benchmark data favours a hybrid model. In a survey of 1,000+ B2B products, **self-serve freemium converted 3–5% (good) to 6–8% (great) to paid, while freemium with sales-assist converted 5–7% to 10–15%**, roughly double ([Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/what-is-a-good-free-to-paid-conversion)). ProductLed's 2022 survey found that product-qualified leads (PQLs) convert at **about 25% on average, 30% at $1–5K ACV and 39% at $5–10K ACV**. Only 34% of companies tracked activation at all ([ProductLed](https://productled.com/blog/product-led-growth-benchmarks)). The field's go-to-market splits the same way. Self-serve tiers exist: NationBuilder starts at $34/mo ([NationBuilder](https://nationbuilder.com/pricing)) and Ecanvasser Core at $99/mo ([Ecanvasser](https://ecanvasser.com/pricing)). Voter-file access, by contrast, is party-mediated and priced by district size ([NY Dems VoteBuilder](https://nydems.org/action/votebuilder)). The Association of State Democratic Parties has "quietly ended its insistence that campaigns only use VAN", which opened room for OpenField, Civitech and others ([The Connector](https://theconnector.substack.com/p/living-with-vanxiety-the-present)). PDI serves IEs, consultants and primary challengers where state-party bylaws protect incumbents ([NY Dems support](https://support.nydems.org/support/solutions/articles/63000258391-other-databases-to-know)). Consultants are paid through partner programs: FullPAC pays **10% of client platform spend for the life of the client** ([C&E directory](https://campaignsandelections.com/consultant-directory/fullpac/)), and Ecanvasser runs reseller and referral programs ([Ecanvasser](https://ecanvasser.com/blog/ecanvassers-partner-program)).

**Inference:** Lynx's current `pending_payment` gate with manual SuperAdmin activation is a sales gate applied to the segment that least needs one. The highest-leverage go-to-market change is to put self-serve checkout on org creation for small orgs and route humans by usage signals. Good triggers would be a voter file above a set size, five or more active canvassers in the first week, or donations approaching the $1,000 compliance unlock. That automatic unlock is already a natural PQL moment. A sales-led track should cover state and county parties, PACs and unions. The wedges, in order, are:

1. Primary challengers and independent expenditures (IEs), who are locked out of party VAN.
2. Unions and advocacy nonprofits, which have no party gatekeeper and run year-round.
3. Consultants, through a multi-client console and a commission of about 10%.

Comparison landing pages against legacy vendors are standard practice ([Impactive vs. Reach](https://www.impactive.io/lp/impactive-vs-reach)). Higher Ground Labs reports that integration is the top complaint in every survey cycle ([HGL](https://highergroundlabs.com/takeaways-from-our-2024-election-tech-survey/)), so VAN, ActBlue and Action Network import/export should be marketed as a way to lower switching risk.

## The volunteer is the viral vector; donor and event surfaces amplify it

The strongest B2B virality is built into the workflow: the job can't be done without involving another person. Two-thirds of Figma's 13M+ monthly active users are non-designers, viewers use it free, and its net dollar retention was **132% (March 2025)** ([Figma S-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1579878/000162828025033742/figma-sx1.htm)). Calendly's version of the loop is a branded booking page plus a post-booking prompt to create your own page ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/chat/h/e375eaca-7657-4575-9b73-660494b47100/)).

The civic data comes mostly from Mobilize. In the 2022 cycle, **140,000 volunteers who invited friends generated 600,000+ additional signups (about 4.3 per inviter)**. Cross-org promotions drove about 15% of signups, social sharing more than 10%, and "nearly 40% of total signups this cycle came from automations and features" ([Mobilize 2022](https://join.mobilize.us/blog/mobilizes-impact-in-2022/)). Its 2020 data adds three more findings ([Mobilize 2020](https://join.mobilize.us/blog/2020-in-review-10-things-weve-learned-about-supporter-management/)):

- Supporters whose first event was volunteer-hosted showed **2x higher retention**.
- By a supporter's fifth signup, 37% of their signups were with people they had acted with before.
- **8% of supporters shown a post-signup donation prompt started a donation**, averaging $35.

Saved-identity networks are the deepest moat in the space. About 40% of donors on typical federal lists were already in ActBlue's Express pool, and Express Lane tests lifted conversion by up to 224.6% ([ActBlue](https://www.actblue.com/?p=2336), roughly 2014 data). WinRed had **5,800+ committees in 2024 (up 177% in four years), 54% of donors new to the platform, and conduiting up 44%** ([WinRed](https://winred.com/blog/2024-cycle)). Givebutter reached 35,000+ nonprofits and $1B+ processed "with almost zero marketing spend", with every donation page exposing its brand ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/04/27/deal-dive-givebutter-is-turning-a-profit-making-tech-for-nonprofits)). Relational organizing is the best *outcome* loop: a friend's text raised turnout by **8.3 points** ([Impactive](https://impactive.io/research-and-data)). However, no growth figures for that loop are published.

K-factor needs realistic framing. K = invites per user × invite conversion. Sustained K > 1 is "mostly a consumer phenomenon", and B2B cycle times are longer, so cutting cycle time matters as much as raising K ([LaunchList](https://app.getlaunchlist.com/blog/viral-coefficient-k-factor-guide)). The research found **no published K-factor for any civic tool**. Team-level activation matters more than user counts. SurveyMonkey had 800+ paid accounts inside single companies, yet enterprise sales kept failing because those users worked in silos ([Elena Verna](https://www.elenaverna.com/p/hey-b2b-i-bet-you-are-measuring-activation)).

**Inference:** Lynx has the Figma structure: one payer, many free doers. It needs three nested loops:

1. **Inside the org (inherent).** A shift invite is a single QR or SMS link with no account friction, and a canvasser logs their first door within minutes.
2. **Across orgs (the real compounding loop).** Give each volunteer a portable identity they own, so they can join their next Lynx campaign in one tap. Pair it with a Calendly-style "Start your own org" prompt for staffers and volunteer leaders, whose sign-up leads straight to paid org creation.
3. **Exposure surfaces.** Add tasteful "Organized with Lynx" branding to shift sign-up pages, gift receipts, shareable end-of-shift impact cards and end-of-cycle reports. Branding must never appear on anything voter-facing at the door. Add a Mobilize-style donation prompt after shift confirmation.

Because WinRed's pool growth concentrated around a top-of-ticket campaign, landing one high-volume anchor campaign per region probably seeds more cross-org volunteer flow than many tiny races. Cross-org data effects should stay at the aggregate-benchmark level, for example a median contact rate by hour across all orgs. Pooling voter-level data would break both Lynx's per-org row-level security (RLS) and voter-file licence terms.

## Activation must be measured at the team level and validated against retention

Define activation as a specific behaviour within a time window that predicts retention. Activation runs through three stages: **setup, aha, habit**. The most common mistake is stopping at setup ([Elena Verna](https://www.elenaverna.com/p/hey-b2b-i-bet-you-are-measuring-activation)). Slack's widely repeated threshold was 2,000 team messages, after which 93% of teams kept using the product. This is mid-2010s data reported second-hand ([Goodreads notes on *The Cold Start Problem*](https://www.goodreads.com/notes/56962799-the-cold-start-problem/84411704-mikko-ikola/435bc888-c6a7-45f1-83c2-6c66617e1ab2)). Median SaaS activation is about **25%**, and for B2B enterprise SaaS about **33%**, with 40% at roughly the 60th percentile ([Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/what-is-a-good-activation-rate)). Verna found that a 3-screen, 9-question onboarding survey cost only about 10% drop-off and did not hurt habit formation. Segmenting by org type, race level and election date at sign-up is therefore cheap.

**Inference:** Proposed Lynx definitions, to be validated Slack-style by comparing retained and churned orgs:

- **Setup:** org paid, voter file imported and geocoded, first turf drawn, one or more volunteers invited.
- **Aha:** the first shift in which three or more volunteers log real `canvass_visits` while the admin watches the live map or heatmap update. A secondary aha for fundraising-led orgs is the first gift recorded and attributed.
- **Habit:** logged shifts in three or more consecutive weeks, or a recurring broadcast cadence.

Because campaigns end on a fixed date, "habit" should be measured within a cycle, and the true retention metric is **re-activation in the next cycle**. Collect the election date at sign-up; every lifecycle trigger hangs off it. The AI layer should be pulled into the aha moment as the briefing that turns the first shift's data into a next-shift plan, rather than left as a separate tab to discover.

## Retention benchmarks reward ARPA, data gravity and seasonal pause, not contracts alone

The benchmarks below set realistic targets.

| Benchmark | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Private B2B SaaS median, 2025 | 101% NRR / 91% GRR | [SaaS Capital 2025](https://www.saas-capital.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/RB32WS1-2025-B2B-SaaS-Retention-Benchmarks.pdf) |
| ACV under $12k | about 98% NRR / 90% GRR | same |
| ACV over $250k | 106% NRR / 95% GRR | same |
| Month-to-month / annual / multi-year | 100/89, 101/90, 103/94 (NRR/GRR) | same |
| Monthly logo churn by ARPA (2022 data) | 6.1% at under $25/mo vs 2.2% at over $500/mo | [ChartMogul](https://chartmogul.com/saas-retention-report/) |
| Share exceeding 100% NRR | 2.7% at ARPA under $10/mo vs 41.1% at over $500/mo | same |
| SaaS churn, voluntary/involuntary | 3.22% total, 1.06% involuntary | [Recurly](https://www.recurly.com/research/churn-rate-benchmarks) |
| AI-native median, 2025 | 40% GRR / 48% NRR; 23% GRR under $50/mo | [Growth Unhinged](https://www.growthunhinged.com/p/the-ai-churn-wave) |

Three conclusions follow from the table. **Price point predicts retention better than almost anything else.** Annual billing adds only a few points of GRR in SaaS Capital's data, though ChartMogul documents a 10–20 point NRR gain for AI products that move to annual billing ([Growth Unhinged](https://www.growthunhinged.com/p/the-ai-churn-wave)). And "GRR must be at least 90%" for performance parity, while NRR drives growth: median growth is **21% at 100–110% NRR vs 30% at 110–120%** ([SaaS Capital](https://www.saas-capital.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/RB32WS1-2025-B2B-SaaS-Retention-Benchmarks.pdf)).

The best-evidenced churn levers are cheap:

- **Involuntary churn** is about a third of SaaS churn. Stripe reports recovering about 55–57% of failed recurring payments, while an independent audit found 25–35% realized recovery ([Flycode](https://www.flycode.com/blog/stripe-smart-retries-adaptive-acceptance-explained-2026)).
- **Pausing** was accepted in **19% of cancellation sessions** across 15M subscriptions ([Churnkey](https://churnkey.co/blog/reduce-churn-subscription-pauses-trial-extensions)).
- **Seasonal businesses use hibernation tiers.** Shopify's Pause and Build plan keeps the store and its data for $9/mo ([Ecorn](https://www.ecorn.agency/blog/shopify-pause-and-build-plan-cost)), and seasonal pause guides recommend setting the resume date to the customer's own return date ([ScaleMath](https://scalemath.com/blog/should-you-let-customers-pause-their-saas-subscription/)).
- **Retention flows should run ahead of known high-churn months**, with annual plans pushed at peak season ([Churnkey seasonality](https://churnkey.co/blog/seasonality-patterns-in-business)).
- **Customer success at this price is digital.** Below about $15k ARR, coverage is typically one CSM per 500+ accounts or none ([MeltingSpot](https://meltingspot.io/en/blog/csm-to-client-ratio-best-practices)).

**Inference:** Lynx should report two retention numbers. The first is in-cycle GRR/NRR, which excludes committees that dissolve on schedule. The second is **cycle-over-cycle reactivation**: the share of orgs, or their former Owners and Managers, who are running a new project within 12 months. The structural fix is a **"Between Cycles" archive tier** of about $10–20/mo, or a discounted annual plan. It would keep voter files, canvass history, door intelligence, donor lists and AI context read-only, with the resume date defaulting to the next filing deadline or primary.

Lynx can offer this cheaply because nothing is ever hard-deleted. Cancel flows should offer "archive until next cycle" before "cancel". Off-cycle value should come from post-election donor retention sequences, list hygiene, issue-advocacy mode and recruiting for the next race. Because the org's free member accounts persist after a committee dissolves, the staffer who moves to a new campaign remains a reachable, warm lead.

Lynx's AI should avoid the "AI tourist" churn pattern. It can do so by tying AI value to recurring workflow events such as shift briefings, debriefs and donor asks, and by never selling AI as a cheap standalone add-on.

## Hybrid pricing lets revenue rise with campaign intensity

Pricing in the market follows four patterns. Field and CRM tools tier by contact count with unlimited users: Ecanvasser says "No per-user fees" and offers a 12-month enterprise minimum and "2 months free" for annual billing ([Ecanvasser](https://ecanvasser.com/pricing)). Texting is per message at about 1.5¢ per SMS at scale ([Scale to Win](https://scaletowin.com/?p=529)). Fundraising is a percentage of money raised, about 3.9% ([HuffPost via Yahoo](https://sg.news.yahoo.com/much-touted-trump-era-fundraising-130000421.html)). Party voter files are sold through sales. NationBuilder charges about 20% more for monthly billing and sells an "Election pack" add-on for +$89/mo ([NationBuilder](https://nationbuilder.com/pricing)).

Across B2B, **hybrid pricing became the most common model in 2025 (41%, up from 27%), while seat-based pricing fell to 15%** ([Growth Unhinged](https://www.growthunhinged.com/p/2025-state-of-b2b-monetization)). For AI, the common pattern is a platform fee plus credits, with unlimited plans "untenable" because of inference costs ([Schematic](https://schematichq.com/monetizing-ai/kyle-poyar-the-state-of-ai-pricing-in-2025)). ICONIQ reports inference costs averaging **23% of revenue**, and consumption pricing for AI rising from 19% to 35% of companies ([SaaStr on ICONIQ](https://www.saastr.com/iconiqs-latest-state-of-ai-report-the-10-most-important-data-points-for-saas-founders)). In older OpenView data, mostly usage-based companies posted top-quartile NRR of 122% vs 109% for those without usage pricing ([Growth Unhinged](https://www.growthunhinged.com/p/the-state-of-usage-based-pricing)).

**Inference:** The recommended structure has four layers:

1. **Org platform fee**, tiered by voter or contact count with unlimited users. This keeps volunteer invites free, which protects the viral loop.
2. **Per-project add-ons**, as today: fundraising, the paid comms tier, and compliance auto-unlocked at $1k raised.
3. **An `ai_module` that includes a monthly credit pool, plus top-up packs.** The current flat entitlement over 80+ Opus-class purposes is the margin trap Poyar and ICONIQ describe.
4. **Metered texting, email and geocoding.** These scale toward election day, when willingness to pay peaks.

Sell "full-cycle" (24-month) and annual plans from August to October of election years, when budgets are flush. Consider an optional percentage-of-raised fee on doorstep and text-to-give gifts as the one revenue line that automatically tracks campaign success. Expansion should come from add-on attach and metered usage, not seats. Pure usage pricing would amplify election swings, so the platform floor matters.

## Federal and carrier rules close off "free for campaigns" tactics

The FEC treats a vendor discount as not being a contribution only "when the discount is made available in the ordinary course of business and on the same terms and conditions to the vendor's other customers that are not political organizations" ([FEC AO 2016-06](https://beta.fec.gov/files/legal/aos/2016-06/2016-06.pdf)). Microsoft's free security offer was approved because it was based on "commercial and not political considerations ... not merely for promotional consideration or to generate goodwill" ([FEC AO 2018-11](https://www.fec.gov/files/legal/aos/2018-11/201811C_1.pdf)). Area 1's first free-to-campaigns request was not approved; it was approved later, once campaigns paid "the same as it charges its qualified non-political clients" ([KrebsOnSecurity](https://krebsonsecurity.com/2019/07/fec-campaigns-can-use-discounted-cybersecurity-services/)).

Texting has its own constraints. Political 10DLC registration has applied since October 1, 2021. Each sending org needs Campaign Verify ($95 per two-year cycle) and registration with The Campaign Registry. Messages must identify the sender, include "Reply STOP to opt-out", and carry no link in the first outbound message ([Tech for Campaigns](https://www.techforcampaigns.org/blog/changes-in-peer-to-peer-texting)). Carriers also require opt-in language on the sender's website ([ClickSend](https://help.clicksend.com/en/articles/46589-political-sms-messaging-on-10dlc)). *Facebook v. Duguid* narrowed the TCPA's autodialer definition, but **statutory damages remain $500–$1,500 per message** ([Faegre Drinker](https://www.faegredrinker.com/en/insights/publications/2021/4/supreme-court-decides-facebook-inc-v-duguid)).

**Inference:** Every Lynx discount, trial, referral credit and archive price must be published once and offered identically to nonprofits and advocacy groups. "First month free for candidates" or "credit for referring another campaign" offered only to committees is the pattern the FEC declined. Counsel should confirm this against 11 CFR 100.52(d) before launch, and the research did not fetch that rule's text. "Organized with Lynx" branding is the vendor's own promotion, so it is a lower-risk loop than discounts. Volunteer bring-a-friend and relational loops should send from the volunteer's own device and never upload personal contacts into the org's database without explicit consent. Any platform-sent text needs per-org 10DLC registration, and Lynx can productize that onboarding as a feature. State mini-TCPA laws, voter-file use restrictions, and whether a free personal volunteer account raises any in-kind question were not researched.

## A prioritized playbook with target metrics

The sequence below favours cheap, well-evidenced levers first, then loops, then network effects. All targets are **inferences** calibrated to the benchmarks above, not sourced predictions.

|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Self-serve paid org creation (replace manual SuperAdmin activation for small orgs); collect election date, org type and race level at sign-up | Manual gate blocks the self-serve segment; onboarding surveys cost about 10% drop-off | Sign-up to paid org 6–10% (freemium with sales-assist "good"); time to paid under 1 day |
| 2 | Dunning, card updater, pre-expiry emails; annual and full-cycle prepay sold Aug–Oct | Involuntary churn is about a third of SaaS churn; recovery 25–57% | Involuntary churn under 0.5%/mo; 50%+ of failed payments recovered; 40%+ of revenue on annual or cycle terms |
| 3 | Team activation: one-link volunteer invite, first door logged within minutes, AI shift briefing at aha | Team-level activation (Verna); median B2B activation about 33% | 40%+ of paid orgs reach aha (3+ canvassers logging visits) within 14 days; validate the threshold against 90-day retention |
| 4 | "Between Cycles" archive tier and pause-first cancel flow; resume date set to the next filing deadline | Pause accepted in 19% of cancel sessions; Shopify-style hibernation | 30%+ of cycle-end orgs archive instead of cancel; 35%+ cycle-over-cycle reactivation |
| 5 | Pricing: contact-tiered platform fee with unlimited users; AI credit pool plus top-ups; metered texting and geocoding | Hybrid pricing at 41%; inference about 23% of revenue | AI gross margin 60%+; in-cycle NRR 105%+; add-on attach 30%+ |
| 6 | PQL routing (large voter files, 5+ canvassers, nearing $1k raised) to human sales; sales-led track for parties, PACs and unions | PQL conversion 25–39% vs single-digit MQL rates | PQL to paid expansion 25%+; 30%+ of ARR from durable orgs within 18 months |
| 7 | Exposure loops: "Organized with Lynx" on shift pages, gift receipts and impact cards; post-shift donation prompt | Mobilize sharing 10%+ of signups; 8% donation-prompt start rate | 5–10% of new orgs attributed to branded surfaces; donation-prompt start rate 5%+ |
| 8 | Portable volunteer identity plus "Start your own org" prompt; consultant multi-client console with about 10% commission | Mobilize bring-a-friend about 4.3 signups per inviter; FullPAC 10% | Org-level K 0.2–0.4 per cycle; 15%+ of new orgs via consultants |
| 9 | Opt-in cross-org aggregate benchmarks (never voter-level pooling); one anchor campaign per region | Mobilize cross-promotions 15% of signups; WinRed's top-of-ticket effect | Benchmark-viewing orgs retain 10+ points better than non-viewers |

Two things are deliberately left off the list. Org-to-org referral credits are excluded unless they are offered identically to non-political customers and cleared by counsel. Any "free for campaigns" tier is excluded for the FEC reasons above.

## Conclusion

The research reframes what "extremely low churn" can mean for Lynx. A candidate committee that dissolves after November isn't a lost customer in the usual sense, and no competitor has escaped that loss. The ones that last turn it into a handoff: the data, the people, or the donor wallet moves on to the next campaign while staying on the platform. So the metric that matters is not monthly logo churn but **how much of each cycle's org data, staff and volunteers comes back in the next one**. Lynx's existing design (paid orgs, free members, append-only history, no hard deletes) is unusually well suited to that metric, provided it's packaged as an archive tier and a portable volunteer identity rather than left as an unused technical property.

The less obvious implication is that the nonprofit and advocacy segment is the legal foundation of the political go-to-market, not just a smoother revenue base. The FEC's "same terms for non-political customers" test means every growth incentive Lynx wants to offer campaigns must first exist as an ordinary commercial offer to nonprofits. Durable orgs and growth tactics are therefore one decision, not two. The largest unknowns are empirical: no vendor publishes cross-cycle reactivation, civic K-factors or post-election churn. Lynx's first competitive advantage could simply be measuring them, using the `canvass_visits` and membership history it already stores.
