# Viral and Network-Effect Growth Loops in Civic, Volunteer, Donor-Facing, and Multi-Sided Organizing Products

Research date: 2026-10-05. Scope: mechanics and numbers that could transfer to Lynx (one org admin pays; volunteers, canvassers, donors, and voters touch the product without paying). Prefer 2020-2026 sources; older sources are flagged where used.

## Q1. Loops that spread through end-users who aren't the buyer (volunteer apps, relational organizing, P2P texting, donor checkout networks, event pages, petitions)

### Takeaway
The strongest measured non-buyer loops in civic tech are (a) **saved-identity checkout networks** (ActBlue Express, WinRed Saved Donor Profile), where a donor who saves a payment method on one campaign becomes a one-click donor for every other campaign on the platform, and (b) **volunteer event networks** (Mobilize), where cross-org promotions, bring-a-friend prompts, and platform-generated suggestions together account for roughly 40% of signups. Relational organizing (volunteers messaging their own contacts) is the most effective *outcome* loop (8.3-point turnout lift) but published data on how it grows the *product* is thin.

### Cited Findings

**Mobilize (volunteer event platform, now part of NGP VAN/Bonterra)**
- 2020: 3,000+ organizations, 4.2 million volunteers, 15 million actions, 340,000 events/online actions created. — [Mobilize 2020 in review](https://join.mobilize.us/blog/2020-in-review-10-things-weve-learned-about-supporter-management/)
- 2020: organizations got "38% more signups from the Mobilize network"; cross-promotion between orgs drove 9% of all platform signups; bring-a-friend prompts produced 14% more signups on average; pre-filling forms raised signups 11%; automated communications yielded 15% more signups; the weekly newsletter drove 53,000 signups in one month. — [Mobilize 2020 in review](https://join.mobilize.us/blog/2020-in-review-10-things-weve-learned-about-supporter-management/)
- 2020: by a supporter's 5th signup, 37% of their signups are with people they've taken action with before (a social-retention loop). — [Mobilize 2020 in review](https://join.mobilize.us/blog/2020-in-review-10-things-weve-learned-about-supporter-management/)
- 2020: 35,000 volunteers hosted events (24% of all events); orgs that used volunteer-hosted events had 5x more volunteer-hosts than admin-hosts (10x for the most prolific); supporters whose first event was volunteer-hosted had 2x higher retention. — [Mobilize 2020 in review](https://join.mobilize.us/blog/2020-in-review-10-things-weve-learned-about-supporter-management/)
- 2020: 8% of supporters shown a donation prompt after signing up started a donation; average gift $35; 84k donations, more than $2M total; volunteers are "2x more likely to donate." — [Mobilize 2020 in review](https://join.mobilize.us/blog/2020-in-review-10-things-weve-learned-about-supporter-management/)
- 2022 cycle: nearly 4,000 organizations, 5.5 million supporters in network, more than 2 million volunteers, nearly 7 million shifts, 19 million unique visitors, 1.5 million new volunteers since Jan 2021. — [Mobilize 2022 impact](https://join.mobilize.us/blog/mobilizes-impact-in-2022/)
- 2022 cycle: Promotions (orgs asking other orgs to cross-amplify their events) drove about 15% of signups; social sharing prompts drove more than 10%; 140,000 volunteers asked friends to join them, producing more than 600,000 additional signups (~4.3 extra signups per inviting volunteer, computed from those two numbers); "nearly 40% of total signups this cycle came from automations and features." — [Mobilize 2022 impact](https://join.mobilize.us/blog/mobilizes-impact-in-2022/)
- Mobilize also reports nearly 20% of signups from automated event suggestions, and the national mobilize.us feed is described as the 5th-largest feed on the platform and often a major signup referrer for partners. — [Mobilize: optimize your digital advocacy toolset](https://join.mobilize.us/blog/optimize-your-digital-advocacy-toolset-with-mobilize/) (via search snippet; not independently fetched)
- Current marketing: "3,000+ mission-driven organizations and over 11 million supporters." — [NGP VAN Mobilize page](https://www.ngpvan.com/solutions/mobilize/) (search snippet)

**Donor checkout networks (ActBlue / WinRed)**
- ActBlue Express Lane mechanism: a donor who saved payment info while giving to one candidate (example: Elizabeth Warren) can give one-click to any other campaign on ActBlue; orgs could match their lists against the Express database free; about 40% of donors on typical federal lists were already in the ActBlue system; Express Lane tests showed conversion increases "up to 224.6%," with higher lift on mobile. Pre-2020 source (~2014, when Express had 618,000 users and ~13,000 new users/month). — [ActBlue: Introducing Express Lane](https://www.actblue.com/?p=2336)
- Express user growth (older, from ActBlue blog): 750,000 by late 2013; 1,301,282 by April 2015; passed 3 million by May 2016. — [ActBlue blog](https://blog.actblue.com/?p=1217); [ActBlue blog, May '16 recap](https://blog.actblue.com/?p=2765)
- 2024 cycle: ActBlue processed $3.3B from 106 million contributions; 9.6 million people gave; more people created Express accounts in the 2024 cycle than all other cycles combined, with 750k+ signing up in a single quarter; "nearly three-quarters of all contributions on ActBlue were made by Express users." — [ActBlue 2024 cycle](https://www.actblue.com/?p=4874) (search snippet). CONFLICT: the same snippet cites "2.9M Express users" as a total, which contradicts the 3M+ total ActBlue reported in 2016; the 2.9M figure is likely new accounts in a period, not a cumulative total. Treat as unverified.
- WinRed 2024 cycle: 5,800+ campaigns/orgs used the platform (177% increase in four years); 4.5 million donors gave $1.8B; 54% of donors were new to WinRed; 46% of donors had a Saved Donor Profile (later stated as 60%); conduiting between committees up 44% vs. prior cycle. — [WinRed 2024 cycle](https://winred.com/blog/2024-cycle)

**Relational organizing / friend-to-friend messaging**
- A text from a friend via Impactive raised likelihood to vote by 8.3 percentage points (2018 midterms, Columbia Data Science Institute). — [Impactive research](https://impactive.io/research-and-data); [Impactive blog](https://impactive.io/blog/largest-ever-study-on-relational-organizing-shows-8-3-percent-increase-in-turnout)
- Community Change 2020 program (Analyst Institute study): contacts reached by a friend/family member through Impactive were 6.5x more likely to sign a petition and 8x more likely to opt into an organizing event. — [Impactive research](https://impactive.io/research-and-data)
- Empower: 12 independent RCTs found large, significant turnout increases; conversations facilitated grew 10x (2016 to 2018), 10x again (2020), and 30x (2024 cycle). Reported 13.2% turnout increase across four states, 17.1% in Ohio with more support structure. — [CallHub relational organizing guide](https://callhub.io/blog/community-organizing/relational-organizing/) (aggregator; figures originate from Empower, not independently verified)
- Impactive's research page does NOT publish contacts-imported-per-volunteer, voter-file match rates, or app user growth. — [Impactive research](https://impactive.io/research-and-data)

**Event pages (consumer analogues)**
- Partiful: each invite is a lightweight landing page sent via iMessage/Instagram/WhatsApp; guests can RSVP without installing, and are nudged to install. Averaged ~500k MAU in Q1 2025, ~400% YoY; added 2 million new users in 2025. — [Sacra on Partiful](https://sacra.com/chat/h/ca6c792b-7d6d-42a9-ae5f-0970a819c66c/); [NoGood on Partiful](https://nogood.io/blog/partiful-marketing-strategy/) (secondary analyses)
- Calendly: branded the interaction (badge during booking and post-booking CTA), so each invitee used the workflow and could create their own page for free; grew from $4.1M ARR (2016) to $8M (2017) on $550K raised. No published % of signups from the loop. — [Startup GTM: Calendly growth story](https://startupgtm.substack.com/p/calendly-growth-story-a-viral-product); [Sacra on Calendly](https://sacra.com/chat/h/e375eaca-7657-4575-9b73-660494b47100/)

**Petitions**
- Change.org: 500,000+ signatures/day; growing by 2M+ new users/month (current marketing); standard playbook is personal outreach to first 5 supporters and off-platform link sharing/embeds. — [Change.org petition guide](https://www.change.org/petition-guides/petition-platforms-compared) (self-reported)
- Change.org's emails have landed in Gmail Promotions since 2018; users receive only 11% of the emails they sign up for (illustrates deliverability ceiling on email-based loops). — search snippet referencing Change.org; original source not fetched
- Change.org does not export signer emails to petition starters (starters get name, country, postal code, date, comments) — the platform keeps the email relationship. — [Change.org llms page](https://llms.change.org/what-happens-after-the-signatures-on-changeorg) (snippet)

### Inferences
- Lynx's closest analogue to ActBlue/WinRed's moat is a **portable volunteer/canvasser identity**: a canvasser who has an account from one Lynx campaign could join another Lynx campaign in one tap. That only creates cross-org value if the identity is the volunteer's (not the org's) and if consent is explicit.
- Mobilize's numbers suggest roughly 40% of signups can come from platform mechanics (promotions + sharing + suggestions) once there is a multi-org network; bring-a-friend at the volunteer level (~4.3 extra signups per inviter) is the most transferable single loop for Lynx shifts/events.
- Post-action donation prompts (Mobilize: 8% initiate, $35 avg) map directly onto Lynx's fundraising module: a volunteer-shift confirmation page with a donate prompt.
- Partiful/Calendly show the loop works when the non-buyer completes a useful action on a public page *without* an account, and sees the brand at the moment of value.

### Gaps
- No published K-factor / viral coefficient for any civic tool (Mobilize, Change.org, ActBlue) was found.
- No data on MiniVAN or Reach growth mechanics; no reliable Reach/Impactive contacts-per-volunteer or voter-file match rates.
- No current (2024-2026) primary ActBlue Express total user count was confirmed; see conflict above.
- No Luma or Eventbrite civic-specific loop data was gathered.

## Q2. Cross-organization network effects (multi-campaign volunteers, consultants managing many clients, data network effects, marketplace/directory effects)

### Takeaway
Cross-org effects are real and measurable in Mobilize (promotions, national feed, repeat co-volunteers) and in donor networks (ActBlue/WinRed shared Express pools; WinRed conduiting up 44%). The consultant/agency channel in political software is mostly monetized through commission/reseller programs (10% of client spend at FullPAC), not organic virality.

### Cited Findings
- Mobilize Promotions (orgs cross-amplifying each other's events) grew from 9% of signups (2020) to about 15% (2022 cycle). — [Mobilize 2020](https://join.mobilize.us/blog/2020-in-review-10-things-weve-learned-about-supporter-management/); [Mobilize 2022](https://join.mobilize.us/blog/mobilizes-impact-in-2022/)
- Mobilize "network" access: orgs reach 4M+ supporters and thousands of orgs, leading to "38% more signups with no extra effort." — [NGP VAN Mobilize](https://www.ngpvan.com/solutions/mobilize/) (snippet)
- ActBlue let orgs match their lists against the shared Express pool at no cost; ~40% of typical federal list donors already in ActBlue (older data). — [ActBlue Express Lane](https://www.actblue.com/?p=2336)
- WinRed: conduiting (committees raising for one another) revenue up 44% cycle-over-cycle; 54% of 2024 donors were new to WinRed, largely driven by one top-of-ticket campaign (Trump) feeding the shared pool used by down-ballot campaigns. — [WinRed 2024 cycle](https://winred.com/blog/2024-cycle)
- FullPAC partner program for political consultants: 10% commission on all direct client platform spend, 2% override on spend of clients from sub-partners, no payout minimum, for the lifetime of the client. — [Campaigns & Elections directory: FullPAC](https://campaignsandelections.com/consultant-directory/fullpac/)
- eCanvasser runs both a regional reseller program for consultants and a referral partner program targeting consultants, agencies and system integrators. — [eCanvasser partner program](https://ecanvasser.com/blog/ecanvassers-partner-program)

### Inferences
- For Lynx, a consultant console (one login, many client orgs) is the natural agency vector; commission norms in the space are ~10% of client spend.
- Lynx data network effects should be framed carefully: aggregate, cross-org benchmarks (e.g., median contact rate by hour) are a plausible "gets better with more customers" feature, but pooling voter-level data across campaigns would conflict with Lynx's RLS-per-org model and with voter-file license terms.
- Shared-pool effects concentrate around marquee customers (WinRed's 54% new donors driven by Trump); seeding Lynx in one high-volume campaign per region may be more valuable than many small ones.

### Gaps
- No published numbers on how many volunteers work for multiple campaigns per cycle.
- No data on the share of NGP VAN/other platforms' sales that come through consultants.
- No measured data-network-effect (model accuracy vs. customer count) for any civic tool.

## Q3. "Powered by" footers, public shareable artifacts, templates/galleries, and B2B referral programs

### Takeaway
"Powered by" branding works best when the non-buyer interacts with the page at a moment of value (donating, booking, RSVPing). Givebutter grew to 35,000+ nonprofits and $1B+ processed with "almost zero marketing spend," using a free, donor-tip-funded model where every donation page exposes the brand to donors. Dropbox's double-sided referral remains the canonical B2B/consumer referral case (35% of signups at peak).

### Cited Findings
- Givebutter: 35,000+ nonprofits, $1B+ processed, ~1% nonprofit market share, profitable, grown "largely with almost zero marketing spend"; then raised $50M from Bessemer (BVP Forge) to fund marketing. — [TechCrunch, Apr 2024](https://techcrunch.com/2024/04/27/deal-dive-givebutter-is-turning-a-profit-making-tech-for-nonprofits)
- Givebutter model: free to nonprofits if donation flows include an optional tip to Givebutter; otherwise a 1-5% platform fee; "95% of donors pay the fee referred to as a tip" (search-snippet claim; source page not fetched, treat as unverified). — [Causeartist on Givebutter](https://causeartist.com/how-givebutter-grew-from-a-college-dorm-to-9b-in-donations/); [The NonProfit Times](https://thenonprofittimes.com/npt_articles/givebutter-gets-50m-private-equity-investment/)
- Calendly: badge on booking + post-booking CTA made each scheduling interaction a top-of-funnel event. — [Sacra on Calendly](https://sacra.com/chat/h/e375eaca-7657-4575-9b73-660494b47100/)
- Partiful: viral lookalike-contest events (e.g., one with nearly 5,000 RSVPs) spread screenshots containing the Partiful link — earned media. — [NoGood on Partiful](https://nogood.io/blog/partiful-marketing-strategy/)
- Dropbox (pre-2020, canonical): launched Sept 2008, 500MB to both sides (16GB cap); signups grew from 100k to 4M in 15 months (3,900%); referrals were 35% of signups at peak, permanently increased signups 60%, 2.8M direct invites in the first 18 months; referrals drove 2.8x more signups than paid channels. — [SaaSquatch: Dropbox referral by the numbers](https://saasquatch.com/dropbox-customer-referral-program-by-the-numbers/) (secondary)

### Inferences
- Lynx surfaces that non-payers see at a moment of value: shift sign-up pages, doorstep "Record gift"/text-to-give receipts, volunteer leaderboards, public event pages. A tasteful "Organized with Lynx" link on those is the Givebutter/Calendly analogue. It should never appear on voter-facing canvass contact (a voter at the door didn't opt in).
- A double-sided referral for *orgs* (e.g., one month credit each) is legally fraught for political buyers (see Q4 — free value to a campaign may be an in-kind contribution) unless the identical offer is open to Lynx's non-political customers (nonprofits/advocacy orgs) on the same terms.
- Shareable impact artifacts (end-of-shift "you knocked 47 doors" cards, campaign end-of-cycle reports) mirror Mobilize's social-sharing prompt, which drove 10%+ of signups.

### Gaps
- No quantified share of signups from "powered by" footers for Givebutter, Donorbox, GoFundMe, Typeform, or Calendly was found.
- No data on template-gallery contributions (Notion/Canva/Figma community) was gathered within the call budget.
- No conversion data on B2B referral programs specific to campaign software.

## Q4. Ethical and legal constraints limiting viral tactics for political software (FEC in-kind, TCPA/10DLC, CAN-SPAM, voter-data privacy)

### Takeaway
The FEC lets vendors give campaigns discounts or free services only when the same terms are offered in the ordinary course of business to non-political customers, and not to curry favor; this rules out "free for campaigns" growth tactics unless matched for non-political customers. Texting loops are gated by carrier 10DLC rules (Campaign Verify, $95/cycle, opt-out language, registration since Oct 1, 2021), and TCPA statutory damages remain $500-$1,500 per message even after *Duguid* narrowed the autodialer definition.

### Cited Findings
- FEC general precedent: a discount does not result in a contribution "when the discount is made available in the ordinary course of business and on the same terms and conditions to the vendor's other customers that are not political organizations or committees." — [FEC AO 2016-06 materials](https://beta.fec.gov/files/legal/aos/2016-06/2016-06.pdf) (search-snippet summary)
- FEC AO 2018-11 (Microsoft): approved free enhanced account security for campaigns because Microsoft would provide it "based on commercial and not political considerations, in the ordinary course of business, and not merely for promotional consideration or to generate goodwill." — [Campaign Legal Center media mention](https://campaignlegal.org/media-mentions/fec-made-under-radar-move-campaign-finance-experts-say-opens-huge-loophole-giving); [FEC AO 2018-11](https://www.fec.gov/files/legal/aos/2018-11/201811C_1.pdf) (PDF not machine-readable here; quote via search snippets)
- FEC AO 2019-12 (Area 1 Security): Area 1's first request (free/low-cost to campaigns) was not approved; the FEC urged resubmission around a capped-price offering; approved because Area 1 charges campaigns "the same as it charges its qualified non-political clients." — [KrebsOnSecurity, July 2019](https://krebsonsecurity.com/2019/07/fec-campaigns-can-use-discounted-cybersecurity-services/)
- FEC AO 2018-12 (Defending Digital Campaigns): allowed a nonprofit to provide free cybersecurity services. — [KrebsOnSecurity](https://krebsonsecurity.com/2019/07/fec-campaigns-can-use-discounted-cybersecurity-services/); critics called the Microsoft opinion a loophole. — [Sludge, Sept 2018](https://readsludge.com/2018/09/10/fec-opens-door-for-corporations-to-give-in-kind-campaign-donations/)
- *Facebook v. Duguid* (Apr 1, 2021, unanimous): an "autodialer" must use a random or sequential number generator to store or produce numbers; TCPA statutory damages are $500-$1,500 per call/text. — [Faegre Drinker](https://www.faegredrinker.com/en/insights/publications/2021/4/supreme-court-decides-facebook-inc-v-duguid); [Gordon Rees](https://www.grsm.com/insight/supreme-court-narrows-autodialer-def-in-major-victory-for-tcpa-defendants-in-facebook-v-duguid/)
- Carrier 10DLC enforcement for political P2P began Oct 1, 2021: each sending org must register with Campaign Verify ($95 per two-year cycle, matched to FEC/state records) and The Campaign Registry; messages must identify sender, include "Reply STOP to opt-out," stay within 3 segments, and avoid links in the first outbound message; unregistered senders risk non-delivery while still paying. — [Tech for Campaigns](https://www.techforcampaigns.org/blog/changes-in-peer-to-peer-texting); [Impactive on 10DLC](https://www.impactive.io/blog/10dlc-for-political-campaigns-and-orgs)
- 10DLC campaigns are not approved without website opt-in language; T-Mobile and AT&T filter unverified political numbers at higher rates. — [ClickSend](https://help.clicksend.com/en/articles/46589-political-sms-messaging-on-10dlc)
- Change.org, as a model of data restraint, does not sell signer data and does not export signer emails to petition creators. — [Change.org](https://llms.change.org/what-happens-after-the-signatures-on-changeorg) (snippet)

### Inferences
- **Lynx pricing implication:** any free tier, referral credit, or discount offered to political campaigns should be offered on identical terms to Lynx's non-political customers (nonprofits, advocacy orgs), documented as ordinary business practice. "Free for candidates" or "credits for referring another campaign" offered only to political committees is the pattern the FEC declined in Area 1's first request. Lynx should get counsel review (cf. CLAUDE.md invariant #6 style caution).
- Corporate in-kind risk also cuts the other way: "powered by" exposure is the vendor's own promotion, not a contribution to the campaign, so it's a lower-risk loop than discounts.
- Volunteer-contact-import (relational) loops should send from the volunteer's own device/number (P2P/relational) and never upload volunteers' personal contacts into the org's shared database without explicit consent. Text loops initiated by the platform need 10DLC registration per org; *Duguid* narrows autodialer exposure but not carrier filtering or state mini-TCPA laws.
- Voter-file data is generally licensed per campaign/committee; any cross-org pooling (shared suppression lists, cross-campaign "warm door" scores) is likely restricted by license and by Lynx's own RLS invariant.

### Gaps
- Could not read the full text of FEC AO 2018-11 (PDF not parseable here); quotes come from secondary sources. The governing rule (11 CFR 100.52(d), "usual and normal charge") was not fetched and should be confirmed.
- CAN-SPAM applicability to political (non-commercial) email was not sourced in this pass; commonly understood to apply only to commercial messages, but Lynx's own marketing emails to campaign staff ARE commercial.
- State mini-TCPA laws (e.g., Florida, Oklahoma) and state voter-file use restrictions were not researched.
- No source found on whether giving volunteers a free personal Lynx account (usable across campaigns) raises in-kind issues; likely not if the volunteer is the user, but unverified.
