# Lynx AI Campaign Advisor
## Comprehensive Question Bank and Capability Map

This question bank is designed for a project-aware campaign advisor inside Lynx. It is not a list of canned responses. Each question represents an intent the advisor should understand, translate into the appropriate data query or analysis, and answer with project-specific evidence.

## 1. What the advisor should do

The advisor should be able to answer four increasingly valuable kinds of questions:

1. Describe: What happened?
2. Diagnose: Why did it happen?
3. Forecast: What is likely to happen next?
4. Recommend: What should the campaign do about it?

The best answers will often combine all four. For example:

"You are projected to finish 18,400 doors short at the current seven-day pace. The shortfall is driven primarily by low staffing on Tuesdays and lower production in Turf 14. Adding three average-performing canvassers to Tuesday shifts, or reallocating two teams from Turf 8 after Thursday, would put the project within 3% of goal. Confidence: moderate, because only 11 production days are available."

## 2. Readiness legend

- Now: Answerable from data represented in the supplied Lynx project screens.
- Derived: Requires calculations, historical comparisons, attribution, anomaly detection, or joining data already or naturally stored by Lynx.
- Integration: Requires a new source, module, or external integration.
- Guarded: Requires careful permissions, privacy controls, compliance rules, or human review.

## 25. Follow-up questions the advisor should understand

The system must preserve conversational context so users can drill down naturally:

- Why?
- Compared with what?
- Show me the last seven days.
- Break that down by team.
- Break that down by turf.
- Exclude today.
- Use only completed shifts.
- What about weekends?
- Who contributed most to that change?
- Is that statistically meaningful?
- How confident are you?
- Show your math.
- What data are you missing?
- What would change your recommendation?
- Give me the conservative version.
- What is the cheapest alternative?
- What is the fastest alternative?
- Turn that into tomorrow's plan.
- Summarize that for the candidate.
- Explain it as if I have 30 seconds.

## 26. Recommended answer contract

Every substantive answer should contain:

1. Direct answer: One or two sentences answering the actual question.
2. Scope: Project, audience, geography, and date range used.
3. Evidence: The most relevant metrics, comparisons, and records.
4. Interpretation: Why the result matters.
5. Recommendation: A specific next step when the user asks for guidance.
6. Expected impact: A quantified range when the data supports one.
7. Confidence: High, moderate, or low, with the reason.
8. Caveat: Missing, stale, incomplete, or externally sourced data.
9. Drill-down: One or two relevant follow-up options.
10. Action boundary: Ask for confirmation before changing schedules, sending communications, filing reports, reassigning turf, or affecting a person.

## 27. Essential behavior and safety rules

- Never invent campaign data, legal requirements, benchmarks, or causal explanations.
- Separate observed facts, calculated results, predictions, and recommendations.
- Use the current user's role and project access before retrieving or revealing data.
- Minimize exposure of voter, donor, staff, signature, and location-level personal information.
- Do not turn productivity analytics into an automatic disciplinary decision.
- Treat QA flags as indicators requiring review, not proof of misconduct.
- For legal and compliance questions, use current jurisdiction-specific sources and show their effective dates.
- Do not make a final determination about petition-signature validity or campaign-finance legality without an authorized human reviewer.
- Identify small samples, missing baselines, stale syncs, or confounding factors.
- When asked "why," distinguish correlation from causation.
- When the user asks for a recommendation, explain the tradeoff and the evidence that would change it.
- Require explicit confirmation before any external or irreversible action.
